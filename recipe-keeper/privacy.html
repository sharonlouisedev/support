Privacy Policy

Recipe Box · Sharon Louise Palmer · Last updated 2 September 2026

This policy explains what information Recipe Box collects, what it does with it, and what it does not do. It applies to the Recipe Box app published by Sharon Louise Palmer (“we”, “us”) on the Apple App Store.

The short version

There is no account, no sign-in and no server of ours. Your recipes live on your device and, if you leave iCloud switched on, in your own iCloud account — which we cannot read. Photographs and PDFs are read on the device itself. The only information that reaches a company other than Apple concerns your subscription, and it is limited to what is needed to confirm that the subscription is genuine and still active.

Information we do not collect

  • No name, email address, phone number or postal address.
  • No account, password or user profile.
  • No contacts, no location, no health data, no browsing history.
  • No advertising identifier. The app shows no App Tracking Transparency prompt because it does not track you across other companies’ apps or websites.
  • No copy of your recipes, your shopping list or your photographs. We have no server to keep them on.

Your recipes, and iCloud

Everything you write, import or scan is stored on your device. If you are signed in to iCloud and have left it enabled for this app, that content is also written to the private area of your iCloud account that Apple reserves for Recipe Box. That is what lets a recipe you saved on your iPhone appear on your iPad.

This part is worth being exact about. That data is held by Apple, under Apple’s privacy policy, inside your own account. It is not sent to us and we cannot read it: a developer has no access to the private database of any user, and there is no dashboard anywhere in which your recipes appear. What we receive is nothing at all.

You can turn the sync off in Settings › your name › iCloud, under the list of apps using iCloud. Turning it off leaves the recipes already on the device exactly where they are; it stops new changes being copied up. Deleting the app removes the local copy, and iCloud content is removed with your iCloud data when you delete it there.

The camera, photographs and PDFs

When you scan a recipe, the text is read by Apple’s Vision framework running on the device. When you import a PDF, it is opened and read on the device. In both cases nothing is uploaded, no image or document is sent anywhere for processing, and the feature works with the phone in aeroplane mode.

You can photograph a page in the app — a page from a book, or a recipe written out by hand. iOS asks your permission the first time, and the camera is used for nothing else: there is no video, no scanning of anything but the page in front of it, and no frame is kept except the pages you actually capture. Those pages are read on the device and become a draft recipe; the first of them is kept as the recipe’s picture, stored the same way the recipe is. Nothing from the camera is uploaded. If you refuse the permission, everything else in the app carries on working and you can still pick a photograph you have already taken.

If you pick an image from your photo library instead, iOS hands the app only the picture you chose — the app cannot see the rest of your library, and it is never asked for access to it.

Importing a recipe from a website

When you paste a link, your device asks that website for the page, directly. The request does not pass through us — there is nothing in the middle, and we never learn which address you pasted or which recipes you save.

The website at the other end does see something, and it is the same thing it would see if you had opened the page in Safari: your IP address, the address requested, and a user-agent string. The app identifies itself as a mobile browser, because a great many recipe sites serve a stripped-down page to anything that does not look like one, and the stripped-down page is the one with the recipe missing. That site’s own privacy policy governs what it does with the request.

What leaves your device

Payments, handled by Apple

All purchases and subscriptions are processed by Apple. We never receive your name, your Apple Account or your payment details, and no payment information passes through us.

Subscription validation and analytics

We use Apphud, a subscription management service, to verify that a purchase is genuine and to measure how many people subscribe. Acting as our processor, Apphud receives:

  • an identifier Apple generates for this app on your device (the identifier for vendor), and an anonymous identifier Apphud creates for itself;
  • the App Store receipt, and subscription events such as a trial starting, a renewal, a cancellation or a refund;
  • basic technical details: device model, operating system version, app version, country and language.

Apphud does not receive your name, your email address, your payment details, or any recipe, photograph, shopping list or note you create in the app. Its handling of this information is governed by its own privacy policy. Processing takes place on servers operated by Apphud in the United States.

Anything you choose to share

A recipe exported as a PDF is produced on your device and goes only where you send it using the iOS share sheet. We are not involved and receive nothing.

Why we process this information

  • To provide the service you paid for — confirming an active subscription is necessary to perform our contract with you.
  • To understand how the app is used in aggregate — our legitimate interest in maintaining and improving it. This never involves identifying an individual.

How long it is kept

Content on your device is kept until you delete it or delete the app. A recipe you delete goes to Recently Deleted and is removed permanently after thirty days, or sooner if you empty it yourself. Subscription records held by Apphud are retained for as long as needed to support the subscription and to meet accounting and tax obligations.

Your rights

Depending on where you live, you may have the right to ask what information is held about you, to have it corrected or deleted, to object to processing, or to receive a copy. Because there is no account, we identify records by device rather than by person.

Email sharonlouise@gmail.com from the device concerned and say what you would like done. We will action it with Apphud and confirm when it is complete. Your recipes are not part of any such request, because we do not hold them — those are yours to delete in the app or in your iCloud settings. Deleting the app stops any further information being sent.

Subscriptions are managed in Settings › Apple Account › Subscriptions. Deleting the app does not cancel a subscription.

Children

The app is suitable for all ages. It asks for no personal information from anyone, including children, and has no messaging, profiles or contact between users.

Selling and sharing

We do not sell personal information and we do not share it for advertising or cross-context behavioural advertising. Apphud is the only third party that receives anything from the app, and only for the purposes described above.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the revised version will be published on this page with a new date at the top.
